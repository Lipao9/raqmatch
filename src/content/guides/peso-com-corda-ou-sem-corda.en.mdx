You find the perfect racquet in a US comparison: 318 g. Then a store listing
in another market says the same racquet weighs 300 g. Who is wrong?

Nobody. **Both numbers are correct — they just measure different things.**
This is one of the biggest sources of confusion for anyone researching
racquets across markets, and understanding it prevents both buying the wrong
frame and discarding the right one.

## The two conventions

- **Strung weight:** the racquet ready to play, strings in. This is the
  convention of US catalogs and reviews — Tennis Warehouse, for example,
  publishes everything strung.
- **Unstrung weight:** the bare frame, as it leaves the factory. It is the
  number printed on the frame itself by the manufacturer and the convention
  in Brazil and much of the rest of the world.

A set of strings weighs roughly **15 to 20 g** depending on gauge and
pattern. In other words: the same racquet looks about 17 g "heavier" in a US
catalog than in a Brazilian listing.

## Why it matters in practice

Seventeen grams sounds small, but in tennis it is a whole category. The
difference between a 285 g and a 300 g frame (unstrung) is exactly the kind of
choice that separates maneuverability from stability — and it is smaller than
the gap between the two conventions.

The classic mistake cuts both ways:

1. **"This racquet is too heavy for me"** — said about the strung figure,
   compared against a current racquet measured unstrung. The candidate looked
   17 g heavier than it plays.
2. **"I bought the same racquet as the review"** — but comparing a listing's
   unstrung number against a review's strung number, the buyer took home a
   frame one category heavier than expected.

## How to compare without getting burned

There is only one rule: **never compare weights without knowing each number's
convention.** In practice:

- Brazilian store listing, or the number printed on the frame → **unstrung**.
- English-language catalog, review or comparison → almost always **strung**.
- When in doubt, subtract ~17 g from the US figure to reach the unstrung
  convention — or add ~17 g going the other way.
- Always compare **within one convention**. Which one does not matter; mixing
  the two is what produces bad decisions.

And remember the published weight describes the frame at factory spec: your
choice of string, a dampener and an overgrip add a few more grams to the
actual playing weight — one more reason not to treat 5 g differences between
listings as decisive.

## How RaqMatch displays weight

Our spec catalog comes from a US source, so the source figure is always the
**strung** weight. For visitors browsing in Portuguese we convert to the local
unstrung convention — and we are transparent about the method:

- When a Brazilian store publishes that racquet's unstrung weight, we use
  **the store's number**, exact.
- When there is no local figure, we derive the unstrung weight by subtracting
  a typical string job and rounding to the nearest 5 g — the granularity
  manufacturers actually publish. Derived values are marked with **≈**,
  because the conversion is only good to about ±5 g: a string job does not
  weigh the same on every frame.
- On racquet pages both conventions appear side by side, each labelled — so
  the number you see here matches both the local listing and the US review.

## The summary

- **Brazilian store / Mercado Livre listing** → unstrung.
- **Printed on the frame** → unstrung.
- **Tennis Warehouse and English-language reviews** → strung.
- **RaqMatch in English** → strung, the convention the specs were sourced in.
