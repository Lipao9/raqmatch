Of all a racquet's specifications, stiffness is the least visible — you cannot
see it on the shelf or feel it holding a still frame — and it matters most for
one specific question: **can your arm handle this racquet for two sets, three
times a week?**

## What the RA scale measures

When the ball arrives, the frame flexes. The RA scale measures resistance to
that flex in a standardized bench test: **the higher the number, the stiffer
the frame**. In our catalog, values run from RA 54 to RA 75 — a narrow-looking
band, but the difference between its ends is enormous in the hand.

The physics of the trade is direct:

- **Stiff frame (high RA):** flexes little, so it returns more energy to the
  ball. "Free" power — and a harsher impact transmitted to hand, elbow and
  shoulder.
- **Flexible frame (low RA):** absorbs part of the impact by flexing. More
  comfort, and a response many players describe as more controllable — but
  the power has to come from your swing.

## Stiffness and pain: what is known

Tennis elbow is multifactorial — technique, playing volume, string, tension
and conditioning all weigh in alongside the frame. But for the racquet's share
of the problem, the biomechanics literature consistently points at **impact
shock and vibration** transmitted to the arm, and stiffer frames transmit
more.

The combination most associated with trouble is exactly what dominates the
entry-level shelf: **a light, stiff, head-heavy frame**. Light, because it
twists more on off-center impact; stiff, because it transmits shock instead of
absorbing it; head-heavy, because it increases the lever on the elbow. Each
factor alone is manageable — all three together, in a player whose technique
is still developing, is the classic recipe for pain.

The obvious still needs saying: **persistent pain is a case for a medical
professional**, and no racquet substitutes for that. What the right choice
does is stop adding to the problem.

## Stiffness does not act alone

Two frames with the same RA can treat your arm differently, because the rest
of the setup participates too:

- **String and tension:** a stiff polyester at high tension hardens any
  racquet; a multifilament at lower tension softens it. If you play with
  pain, the string is the cheapest adjustment before changing frames.
- **Weight:** mass absorbs shock. A slightly heavier frame (within what you
  can swing comfortably) tends to be gentler than an ultralight at the same
  RA.
- **Contact point:** off-center impact transmits more twist — part of the
  comfort of larger heads comes from forgiving those hits.

## The summary

If free power is the priority and your arm has never complained, high RA is a
legitimate trade. If you have ever felt your elbow after playing, **look at
RA 62 and below before any other spec** — and treat weight and string as part
of the same package.
