## Start from your game, not from the catalog

Before looking at a single spec, answer three questions honestly:

- **Is your swing short or long?** A player with a compact swing needs the
  racquet to supply power; a player with a full, fast swing needs it to not
  overshoot.
- **What is missing from your game right now?** Power, control, spin and
  comfort each ask for different specs — and some of them are mutually
  exclusive.
- **Does your arm complain?** Elbow or shoulder pain reorders every other
  priority. If that is you, also read the guide on
  [stiffness and arm pain](/guides/rigidez-ra-e-dor-no-braco).

## The four specs that matter

### Head size: power and forgiveness

Head size, measured in square inches, is tennis's most direct trade-off:
**bigger head = more power and more forgiveness; smaller head = more control
and precision**. A larger head has a bigger sweet spot and longer strings that
return more energy — and it forgives off-center contact.

In the catalog, head sizes run from 93 to 120 in², and{" "}
<CatalogStat stat="shareHead100" label="are 100 in² or larger" />. The
practical ruler:

- **98 in² or less** — for players who already find the center consistently
  and want to place the ball, not push it.
- **100 in²** — the modern middle ground; most current performance racquets
  live here.
- **104 in² or more** — ease first: more free power and more forgiveness in
  exchange for precision.

### Weight: stability versus maneuverability

<SpecScale spec="weight" value={300} label="Example: 300 g on the catalog's ruler (strung)" unit="g" />

A heavy racquet resists the incoming ball better — the frame does not twist on
impact — but demands early preparation and good conditioning. A light racquet
whips around fast and saves you at the net, but it flutters and twists against
heavy balls.

One detail that trips up shoppers comparing across markets: **the same frame
has two published weights**, strung (the US convention) and unstrung (the
convention in Brazil and much of the world), roughly 17 g apart. Before
comparing numbers between stores, read
[strung vs unstrung weight](/guides/peso-com-corda-ou-sem-corda).

### Stiffness: free power, with interest

Stiffness (the RA scale) measures how much the frame flexes at impact. A stiff
frame returns more energy to the ball — and transmits more shock to your arm.
A flexible frame is more comfortable and gives a stronger sense of control,
but asks your swing to supply the power. The catalog runs from RA 54 to
RA 75; the median sits at{" "}
<CatalogStat stat="medianStiffness" label="in the catalog" />. It is the
deciding spec for anyone with a history of arm pain — the
[stiffness guide](/guides/rigidez-ra-e-dor-no-braco) covers exactly that.

### String pattern: spin versus control

The pattern (e.g. 16x19) counts main and cross strings. **An open pattern
(16x19) lets the strings move more: more spin and more power. A dense pattern
(18x20) responds more predictably and directly: more control**, with a flatter
trajectory. 16x19 dominates the current market precisely because the modern
game is built on topspin.

## What you can ignore

- **"It's what that tour player uses."** Pro racquets are frequently modified,
  much heavier versions of the retail frame wearing the same paint. The spec
  that matters is the one on the label, not the poster.
- **Branded technologies.** Every brand renames similar concepts each
  generation. The four specs in this guide describe actual behavior; the rest
  is packaging.
- **The color.** Seriously.
